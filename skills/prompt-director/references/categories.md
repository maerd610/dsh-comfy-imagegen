# Categories

The paragraph shape follows the category, not the user's habit. Infer the category from the brief; say which one you inferred only when the choice changes the output.

| | Medium noun | Frame strategy | Text policy | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| poster | "poster" + print process | regions, type blocks, negative space | expected; plan the type block | 400–550 |
| illustration | "illustration" + medium | single subject or shallow scene | only if asked, or as a small sign | 250–350 |
| photograph | "photograph" + light discipline | single subject, real space | signage allowed if the place owns it | 250–350 |
| product | "product photograph/rendering" | one object, one surface, one shadow | none unless packaging copy given | 250–350 |
| character | "character design sheet" | full body + a second view | name/label optional | 300–400 |

## poster

Wants: one dominant image or one dominant shape, a type zone, a visible grid, edges that hold.

Walk it as regions: background, top band, body, bottom band, whole. Reserve the corner or the top where copy will live — describe that zone's colour and emptiness, so the type has somewhere to sit.

Traps: describing a poster as if it were a painting (no edges, no type block); putting the copy in the middle where it fights the image; adding a full paragraph of body copy nobody asked for. One or two readable strings is a poster. Six is a page.

Ratio instinct: vertical 2:3 or 3:4, tall 9:16 for phone formats, 1:1 for emblem-like posters.

## illustration

Wants: a single readable gesture, a coherent palette, atmosphere carried by value rather than by many objects.

Walk it as a subject: background and falloff, pose and placement, head, body, hands, edges. One asymmetry does the storytelling work — a strand of hair, a sleeve pulled down, a collar standing on one side. Invent freely inside the stated content; do not invent objects.

Traps: turning illustration into photography by importing lens language; filling empty space because the corner looks lonely (use gradient, texture, or a shadow instead).

Ratio instinct: 3:2 or 4:5.

## photograph

The only category where camera vocabulary is legitimate, and only when the user asks for a photographic result. Even then, prefer the light and the lens **effect** over the equipment: "a short telephoto compresses the shelves behind her" tells the renderer more than "85mm f/1.4".

Wants: a plausible place, believable light, one unguarded gesture.

Traps: stock photography vocabulary; perfect symmetry; a person lit from three directions at once.

Ratio instinct: 3:2 horizontal, 2:3 vertical, 16:9 for editorial.

## product

Wants: the object's silhouette, its material behaviour, its contact with the ground, one controlled highlight. Background is a function, not scenery.

Walk it as: background and its gradient, object and its placement, surface, edges, highlight, shadow, reflection. State the material — matte ceramic, brushed aluminium, frosted glass — because material is the whole sell.

Traps: adding props that imply a lifestyle (coffee beans, a linen napkin); a shadow that contradicts the light; packaging copy the user never supplied.

Ratio instinct: 1:1 for catalogue, 4:5 for social, 3:2 for editorial.

## character

Wants: design, not personality. Costume logic, silhouette, a readable silhouette at a glance, one identifying detail.

Walk it as: neutral ground, full-body front view, then a second view (back, profile, or an action pose) if the user allows one paragraph; hair, face, garment layers, footwear, held object.

Traps: writing a biography instead of an outfit; describing a mood instead of a stance; leaving out the hands, which is where character design usually breaks.

Ratio instinct: 2:3 or 9:16 for sheets, 1:1 for head studies.

## Mixed and conflicting

Poster first when copy is required. Illustration when the user says "picture" but names no type. Product when the object is the subject even inside a poster — then it is a product poster: regions from poster, material discipline from product.

Announce a conflict in one line below the prompt, never inside it.

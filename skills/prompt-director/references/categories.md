# Categories

The paragraph shape follows the deliverable, not the user's habit. Infer the category from the brief; name it only when the choice changes the output.

| | Medium noun | Reading mode | Density (units / strings) | Text policy | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| poster | "poster" + print process | used, regions | 15–25 / 5–20 | expected; plan the type zone | 400–550 |
| illustration | "illustration" + medium | looked at, single subject | 8–14 / 0–3 | only if asked, or one small sign | 250–350 |
| photograph | "photograph" + light discipline | looked at, single subject | 8–14 / 0–3 | signage the place owns, nothing invented | 250–350 |
| product | "product photograph / rendering" | used, single object | 12–18 / 0–4 | none unless packaging copy is given | 250–350 |
| character | "character design sheet" | used, regions | 15–25 / 2–6 | name and labels optional | 300–400 |

Deliverables outside the five, and where they land: screenshot, page, package, slide, comic panel, diagram, logo, pattern, texture. A screenshot is a layered frame and reads as `poster` for density purposes; a comedic full-page comic is a read frame and runs 500–700 words, one paragraph per panel group; a logo is a used frame with a single mark and almost no text.

An **edit** is not a sixth category. It inherits the category, reading mode and register of the picture it changes, and only its length changes — 120–220 words in caption form, 40–120 in instruction form. When an edit turns a photograph into a poster, or adds a panel to a page, the new object's category governs the region you describe.

## Choose the reading mode first

The same subject fails in opposite directions depending on this axis.

- **looked at** — atmosphere carries it. Light, material and distance do the work; text is a small sign at most. A photograph with six headlines is broken.
- **used** — hierarchy carries it. Every zone has a job, type is prominent, whitespace is planned. A poster with no headline is broken.
- **read** — information carries it. Strings are numerous, labels explicit, and the count of text blocks is part of the design.

When the user's words and the deliverable disagree — "a poster" with no copy, "a product shot" with no surface — follow the user, then name the tension in one line below the prompt.

## poster

Wants: one dominant image or shape, a type zone with room, a visible grid, edges that hold.

Walk it as regions: background, top band, body left to right, bottom band, whole. Reserve the corner or the top where copy will live — describe that zone's colour and emptiness, so the type has somewhere to sit.

Traps: describing a poster as if it were a painting (no edges, no type block); copy in the middle where it fights the image; a paragraph of body copy nobody asked for. One or two readable strings is a poster. Six is a page.

Ratio instinct: vertical 2:3 or 3:4, tall 9:16 for phone formats, 1:1 for emblem-like posters.

## illustration

Wants: one readable gesture, a coherent palette, atmosphere carried by value rather than by many objects.

Walk it as a subject: background and falloff, pose and placement, head, body, hands, edges. One asymmetry does the storytelling work — a strand of hair, a sleeve pulled down, a collar standing on one side. Invent freely inside the stated content; do not invent objects.

Traps: importing lens language and turning an illustration into photography; filling a corner because it looks lonely (use gradient, texture or a shadow instead); a register stated in the first sentence and never paid off in surface, palette or brushwork.

Ratio instinct: 3:2 or 4:5.

## photograph

The only category where camera vocabulary is legitimate, and only when the user asks for a photographic result. Even then, prefer the light and the lens **effect** over the equipment: "a short telephoto compresses the shelves behind her" tells the renderer more than "85mm f/1.4". A period or process adjective — digicam, 35mm, Polaroid, phone snapshot — is a register, and belongs in the opening sentence, not in a gear list.

Wants: a plausible place, believable light, one unguarded gesture.

Traps: stock-photography vocabulary; perfect symmetry; a person lit from three directions at once; sensor artefacts listed as adjectives instead of as consequences ("faded bluish-grey, blown highlights, visible digital grain" beats "retro, film-like").

Ratio instinct: 3:2 horizontal, 2:3 vertical, 16:9 for editorial.

## product

Wants: the object's silhouette, its material behaviour, its contact with the ground, one controlled highlight. Background is a function, not scenery.

Walk it as: background and its gradient, object and its placement, surface, edges, highlight, shadow, reflection. State the material — matte ceramic, brushed aluminium, frosted glass — because material is the whole sell.

Traps: props that imply a lifestyle (coffee beans, a linen napkin); a shadow that contradicts the light; packaging copy the user never supplied; a hero object described with more energy than the surface it stands on.

Ratio instinct: 1:1 for catalogue, 4:5 for social, 3:2 for editorial.

## character

Wants: design, not personality. Costume logic, a readable silhouette at a glance, one identifying detail.

Walk it as: neutral ground, full-body front view, then a second view (back, profile, or an action pose) if one paragraph can carry it; hair, face, garment layers, footwear, held object.

Traps: a biography instead of an outfit; a mood instead of a stance; leaving out the hands, which is where character design usually breaks; an anime face described only in style words with no linework, no eye construction, no shadow logic.

Ratio instinct: 2:3 or 9:16 for sheets, 1:1 for head studies.

## Mixed and conflicting

Poster first when copy is required. Illustration when the user says "picture" but names no type. Product when the object is the subject even inside a poster — then it is a product poster: regions from poster, material discipline from product. A layered frame (screenshot over a scene) takes the density of `read` and the type discipline of `poster`.

Announce a conflict in one line below the prompt, never inside it.
